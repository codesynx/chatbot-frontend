Diabetes (D)

Our fundus analysis service helps detect signs of diabetic retinopathy (DR), a serious complication of diabetes that affects the retina due to damage to small blood vessels.

Key Signs of Diabetic Retinopathy

Microaneurysms

- Early signs of DR include small dilated blood vessels (aneurysms) in the retina.
- They appear as tiny red dots and may rupture, causing hemorrhages.

Hemorrhages

- Small dot or blot hemorrhages can appear in different layers of the retina.
- Larger hemorrhages may occur in the vitreous body, leading to sudden vision loss.

Macular Edema

- Fluid accumulation in the central part of the retina (macula), leading to impaired central vision.
- One of the primary causes of vision loss in diabetes.

Neovascularization

- The body attempts to compensate for oxygen deficiency by creating new abnormal blood vessels.
- These vessels are fragile and can rupture, causing severe complications.

Hard Exudates

- Bright spots on the retina caused by leakage of lipids and proteins.
- They can impair vision if located in the central area.

Analysis Results

  • Patients are most commonly in the age range of 35 to 70 years
  • Images show noticeable vascular changes and hemorrhages
  • Categorized into non-proliferative, proliferative, and macular edema stages

What Does a "Diabetes" Result Mean?

If your analysis result indicates signs of diabetes, it means that retinal changes characteristic of diabetic retinopathy have been detected. This could suggest an early or progressing stage of the disease.

Recommendations:

  • Regularly monitor blood sugar levels
  • Annual ophthalmologist checkups
  • Possible laser treatment or injections
  • Control blood pressure and cholesterol
