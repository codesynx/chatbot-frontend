Normal (N)

Our analysis of fundus images labeled as "Normal" (N) indicates that patients with this classification do not exhibit any pathological changes in the retina, optic nerve, or ocular blood vessels.

Key Characteristics of a Normal Fundus

Clear Optic Disc Boundaries

- The optic nerve has smooth, well-defined edges without signs of swelling or atrophy.
- The disc color is pale pink, indicating healthy blood supply.

Even Distribution of Retinal Blood Vessels

- Arteries and veins are naturally arranged, without pathological dilation, narrowing, or tortuosity.
- There are no signs of microaneurysms, hemorrhages, or neovascularization.

Uniform Macular Structure (Central Retinal Zone)

- The fovea (central pit) is clearly defined, with no signs of swelling or dystrophy.
- No changes indicative of macular degeneration are present.

Consistent Retinal Color and Structure

- The retina has a uniform color, without dark or light spots that could indicate pathological processes.
- The pigment epithelium does not contain areas of hypo- or hyperpigmentation.

Results of Our Analysis

What Does a "Normal" Result Mean?

If the system classifies your fundus image as "Normal," it means that no obvious signs of disease have been detected. However, it is important to remember that our AI service serves as an assistive tool, and the final diagnosis should always be made by an ophthalmologist.
