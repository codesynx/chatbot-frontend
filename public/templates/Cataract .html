Cataract (C)

Cataract is a condition characterized by the clouding of the lens, leading to vision impairment. It develops gradually and can result in complete vision loss if not treated in time.

Key Signs of Cataract

Lens Opacification

- In fundus images, cataracts may appear as blurriness or reduced clarity of retinal details due to the cloudy lens obstructing light passage.
- In advanced cases, the red reflex from the fundus may be completely absent.

Reduced Contrast

- Due to lens clouding, the fundus image may appear less contrasted, particularly in the macular (central retinal) region.

Light Scattering

- Fundus images of cataract cases may sometimes show uneven glare caused by distorted light transmission through the cloudy lens.

Analysis Results

  • Cataracts most commonly develop in individuals over 50 years old but may also occur in younger patients due to trauma or congenital factors.
  • Fundus images with cataracts often exhibit reduced visualization quality, making it challenging to diagnose other eye diseases.
  • The primary risk factor is age-related changes, but cataracts can also be linked to diabetes, ultraviolet exposure, eye injuries, and prolonged steroid use.

What Does a "Cataract" Result Mean?

If your analysis result indicates cataracts, it means that lens opacities are present, potentially affecting vision. In early stages, cataracts may not cause significant discomfort, but they progressively worsen, leading to severe vision impairment.

Recommendations:

  • Regularly visit an ophthalmologist, especially if you are over 50 or have risk factors.
  • Wear UV-protective glasses, as prolonged sun exposure accelerates cataract development.
  • Maintain a diet rich in antioxidants (vitamins C and E) to slow cataract progression.
  • If vision impairment becomes significant, cataract surgery with an intraocular lens (IOL) replacement is the only effective treatment option.
