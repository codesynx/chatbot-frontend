Glaucoma (G)

Glaucoma is a chronic disease characterized by increased intraocular pressure (IOP), leading to optic nerve damage and gradual vision loss. Without timely treatment, glaucoma can result in irreversible blindness.

Key Signs of Glaucoma

Optic Disc Changes

- Enlargement of the optic disc excavation (cupping) – the central part of the optic disc becomes wider, indicating nerve fiber damage.
- The optic disc borders may appear blurred or atrophied.

Retinal Nerve Fiber Layer Thinning

- Thinning of nerve fiber layer in upper/lower zones
- Visible structural changes in retinal layers

Vascular Changes

- Blood vessels around the optic nerve may change direction, narrow, or become less distinct.
- The optic disc may appear paler due to tissue atrophy.

Visual Field Defects

- In later stages, patients experience tunnel vision (loss of peripheral vision) and dark spots in their visual field.
- Fundus image analysis often reveals changes in vascular patterns and optic nerve structure, characteristic of nerve cell loss.

Analysis Results

  • Glaucoma most commonly develops in individuals over 40 years old but can also occur at a younger age.
  • Fundus images of glaucoma cases reveal characteristic optic disc changes, RNFL thinning, and vascular abnormalities.
  • Patients with glaucoma often have increased intraocular pressure, but some cases occur with normal IOP (normal-tension glaucoma).

What Does a "Glaucoma" Result Mean?

If your analysis result indicates glaucoma, it may suggest optic nerve damage associated with increased intraocular pressure or impaired blood supply. This is a serious condition that often progresses without symptoms until advanced stages, making early diagnosis crucial.

Recommendations:

  • Regularly monitor intraocular pressure and undergo ophthalmologic examinations.
  • If glaucomatous changes are detected, seek medical attention immediately—early treatment can help preserve vision.
  • If glaucoma is confirmed, your doctor may prescribe eye drops, laser treatment, or surgery to reduce intraocular pressure.
  • People over 40, especially those with a family history of glaucoma, should have preventive eye exams annually.
