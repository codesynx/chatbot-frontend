Hypertension (H)

Hypertensive retinopathy is damage to the retinal blood vessels caused by high blood pressure. Prolonged hypertension can lead to vascular changes in the fundus, impaired blood circulation, and optic nerve damage, increasing the risk of vision loss.

Key Signs of Hypertensive Retinopathy

Arterial Narrowing

- Hypertension causes vascular spasms, leading to arterial narrowing and increased tortuosity.
- In fundus analysis, this appears as uneven, thin, and winding blood vessels.

Arteriovenous Crossings

- Due to high blood pressure, arteries begin to compress veins at crossing points, potentially disrupting blood flow.
- In fundus analysis, this looks like vein constriction at arterial crossing points.

Retinal Hemorrhages

- High blood pressure may cause ruptures in small blood vessels, resulting in pinpoint or linear hemorrhages.
- In fundus images, these appear as dark or red spots of varying shapes.

Optic Disc Swelling

- In advanced stages of hypertensive retinopathy, optic disc swelling occurs, leading to blurred disc margins in fundus analysis.

Analysis Results

  • Patients with chronic hypertension often exhibit vascular changes, hemorrhages, and retinal swelling.
  • Hypertension also increases the risk of developing glaucoma and age-related macular degeneration.
  • Blood pressure control is crucial, as retinal damage is often an early sign of hypertensive disease.

What Does "Hypertension" Result Mean?

If your analysis result indicates hypertension, it means your retinal blood vessels are under stress due to high blood pressure. This can lead to vision deterioration and serious complications.

Recommendations:

  • Monitor blood pressure—keep it within normal limits(<120/80 mmHg)
  • Have regular ophthalmologist check-ups, especially if diagnosed with hypertension.
  • Include potassium, magnesium, and omega-3 in your diet (bananas, nuts, fish) and reduce salt intake.
  • Consult a cardiologist and physician for appropriate antihypertensive therapy if fundus changes are detected.
  • In advanced hypertensive retinopathy, laser treatment or medication may be necessary to prevent complications.
